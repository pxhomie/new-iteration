Get Started

Most design engagements stall in revisions before anything ships.

I work in short cycles, so you see real screens in days and we correct course while it's still cheap.

Phase  01

Understand

Your product, your users, and the constraint you're actually up against. The brief and the real problem are usually two different things, and finding the gap is worth an hour.

02

Direction

Before production, I design one key screen in two or three visual directions. You pick one and we lock the language for everything after. This is the step that saves weeks of revisions later.

03

Build

Screens, states, components, prototypes. Check-ins every few days instead of one big reveal, so nothing drifts far from what you had in mind.

04

Ship & Iterate

Clean files, documented components, support through implementation. Then adjustments based on what real users actually do, because the first version is a hypothesis.

    FAQ

    What exactly do you do? +

    Two things. I design digital products: SaaS platforms, crypto apps, mobile apps, dashboards. And I design websites and landing pages built around conversion. The first comes from 5 years of building products from zero, the second from 1.5 years at a US CRO agency.

    Who do you usually work with? +

    Founders and small product teams. Sometimes it's a pre-launch product that needs a real visual direction and a working flow. Sometimes it's a live site that gets traffic and doesn't convert. The common thread is a team that wants to ship rather than explore.

    Are you an agency? +

    No. It's me. You talk directly to the person doing the work. When something falls outside my range, I say so instead of stretching.

    What industries have you worked in? +

    SaaS, crypto, fintech, iGaming, and a lot of general web work. Crypto specifically: I've worked on AirNFTs and PerpMate, so wallets, transactions, and onboarding people who have never used one are familiar territory.

    How do retainers work? +

    A fixed amount of design time each month, used however you need it. Priorities get set at the start of each cycle. It suits teams with steady design needs but not enough to justify a full-time hire.

    Do you offer fixed-price projects? +

    Yes, when the scope is clear enough to price honestly. When there are real unknowns, a sprint protects both of us better than a number pulled from optimism.

    What's your minimum engagement? +

    A two-week sprint. Enough to deliver something real, and enough for both of us to tell whether this is working.

    Can I switch between formats? +

    Yes. Most clients start with a sprint and move into a retainer or a full project after.

    How fast can you start? +

    Usually within a few days to a week depending on what's in progress. Rush availability comes up, so it's worth asking.

    What do you need from me to begin? +

    Whatever you have: existing designs, a rough brief, references you like, or just a conversation about the problem. If you don't have references, I can put together a moodboard so we align on direction before real work starts.

    How often do we talk? +

    Your preference. Weekly calls, async comments in Figma, or a mix. Either works as long as feedback comes back within a reasonable window so the work doesn't stall.

    What does the first week look like? +

    Understanding the product, mapping the flows that matter, and putting one key screen into two or three visual directions. By the end of it you're looking at real design, not a research deck.

    Do you work with existing design files? +

    Often. Picking up someone else's Figma, cleaning up components, and pushing the visual layer forward without rebuilding from scratch is a normal part of the job.

    How do you measure whether the design worked? +

    For products: activation, onboarding completion, task success, retention. For websites: conversion, whatever the primary action is. We agree on the number before starting so there's nothing to argue about later.

    Do you run A/B tests? +

    I design them and interpret the results. Running them needs your analytics and dev setup, but structuring a test properly is a design problem as much as a technical one, and that's what I did daily at Spiralyze.

    What do I get at the end? +

    Figma files with organized components, documented states, an interactive prototype where useful, and handoff specs. All of it yours.

    Do you help after handoff? +

    Yes. I review the built version against the design, and stay available for adjustments once real users arrive.
