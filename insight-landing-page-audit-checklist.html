Landing page audit checklist: 10 things to check before a redesign.

An hour with this checklist usually finds the leak a three-month redesign would have repainted.

February 20, 2026

Before any redesign conversation, I run the same audit — ten checks, one hour, no tools beyond a phone and analytics access. It almost always relocates the problem from “the design is old” to something specific and fixable this week.

What a Landing Page Audit Is

An audit answers one question: where exactly does this page lose people? Not “is it pretty” — where do visitors stop believing, stop understanding, or stop waiting. That’s measurable, and it maps to specific screens and sentences, not to taste.

Above the Fold: The First Five Checks

The first screen carries most of the conversion weight, so it gets half the checklist. Headline about the visitor’s problem, not the company’s history. One primary action, visually unmissable. Proof within the same glance. Load under two seconds on a phone. And the ten-second test: a stranger can say what this is and what to do next.

Cover the logo and read the page. If the headline could sit on any competitor’s site unchanged — that’s the leak, and no redesign fixes a sentence.

Message and Proof

Below the fold the audit follows the argument: every claim needs its evidence within a scroll. Numbers beat adjectives, named clients beat logos, one concrete case beats six abstract benefits. Check the order too — pages that convert answer objections in the sequence visitors raise them, not in the sequence the sitemap was drawn.

Speed and Mobile

Then the unglamorous killers. Real-device load time on mobile data, layout shift while fonts and images arrive, tap targets, and the form itself: every field past name and email costs conversions — each one has to justify its existence.

The 10-Point Checklist

#CheckRed flag
1Headline names the visitor’s problemIt describes the company instead
2One clear primary CTAThree buttons competing
3Proof next to the askTestimonials buried at the bottom
4Mobile LCP under ~2.5sHero image is a 4MB PNG
5Passes the 10-second test“What do they actually sell?”
6Claims ordered by objectionsFeatures listed by org chart
7Numbers, names, specifics“World-class”, “innovative”
8Form asks the minimumNine required fields
9No layout shift on loadText jumps as fonts swap
10Next step after submit is obviousGeneric “thank you” dead end

Score it honestly, fix the reds in order, remeasure in two weeks. Then decide whether you still need that redesign.
