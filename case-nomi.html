An AI tutor built for studying together.

The challenge

Put a personal tutor in every student’s pocket.

That was the brief for Nomi. The tutor is on call around the clock, follows the student’s own level and subjects, and answers with a guiding question instead of a ready solution — so the student ends up understanding the topic instead of copying it. Support that doesn’t run out after one explanation.

The research

The idea started as a Discord chatbot — but Discord turned out to be too complex for schoolkids.

Research covered ten chat apps, analyzed one by one. The project started with a broad idea — an AI chatbot living in Discord — but for schoolkids Discord felt too complex. So the direction narrowed step by step: a standalone mobile app, designed around the student.

The wireframes

The app was built as a chat first — everything else had to earn its place around it.

In wireframes we laid out the four screens that carry the product: onboarding, Home, the tutor chat and the quiz. The rule was simple — a student should reach the tutor in two taps from opening the app, and inviting a friend should be one more. Everything that didn’t serve those two paths was cut before any visual work started.

The concepts

Two visual directions: strict monochrome — and the same app with a touch of blue.

The first concept is clean black, white and gray: calm and close to the brand. The second keeps the same layout, but adds a little blue and livelier details for a younger audience. The final UI took the best of both — calm greys and near-black, a little color for accents, and the Inter typeface, which handles the app’s many languages.

The solution

The product was never the answer — it was how a student gets to it.

The app stands on two ideas, and both were there in the very first wireframes. First — a tutor that teaches by asking guiding questions instead of giving the answer right away. Second — studying together: friends answer the tutor’s questions in the same chat, and that early concept made it into the release as a branded feature. Important buttons are easy to spot, everything else stays quiet, and all the features connect into one clear user flow the developers could build from.

The screens

The invite card and the profile turn studying into something students come back to.

Onboarding asks three simple questions — who you are, what level you study at, which subjects you need — and sets up a tutor tuned to the answers. In the chat the student picks a topic and earns points for right answers, so even a solo session keeps score. On Home, a “Participate in Multiplayer Quiz” card sits next to the subject recommendations — one tap invites a friend into a quiz against the clock, the AI keeps the game fair, and at the end the group votes for the best player. Every session feeds the Profile: points, victories, subjects covered, and badges for milestones. Together they give a student a reason to come back — even without a new question to ask.

The results

Nomi shipped as a private beta — with its two core ideas intact.

  • Shipped: multiplayer went from a rough wireframe
    to a branded feature in the release build
  • Points, badges and the profile shipped alongside —
    progress a student can actually see
  • Handoff was a working system: UI kit, chat components,
    and animated flows developers could build from directly
  • A note for the future: leaderboards motivate the winner —
    and can quietly demotivate everyone else
